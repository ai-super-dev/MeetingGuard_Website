Your calendar knows about the investor call at 10:00. So does your phone. And still, at 10:04, you are answering an email while the call starts without you.

The problem is not that you forgot. The problem is that the reminder was a notification.

## A notification is designed to be ignored

Notifications arrive all day: messages, likes, delivery updates, the same app asking you to rate it. Your phone groups them, silences them and hides them, because otherwise they would be unbearable. A meeting reminder lands in the same pile and looks exactly like everything else. One swipe and it is gone.

That works for news. It does not work for the meeting that pays your salary.

## An alarm asks for an answer

An alarm is different. It takes over the screen, makes a sound and waits until you respond. You cannot swipe it into a pile. You snooze it, dismiss it or join the call.

That is what MeetingGuard does before every meeting:

- A full-screen alarm, over the lock screen, with sound and the meeting read aloud.
- Snooze for 5 or 10 minutes, or dismiss in one tap.
- Follow-ups that continue until you respond.
- Join now, straight from the alarm.

## Not every meeting needs a siren

A daily standup is not a board meeting. So you choose the intensity for each one: gentle for a quiet heads-up, standard with sound and vibration, or critical for the full-screen alarm with voice readout.

## It keeps working when you are not looking

Alarms are scheduled on your phone. They ring with the app closed and are set again after a restart. On Android 14 and later you allow full-screen alarms once, and the app shows you exactly where.

## The result

You stop relying on remembering to check. The phone tells you, in a way you cannot miss, and the call link is one tap away.
